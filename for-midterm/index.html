Step 8 of 8 • Final Distinction

Thesis vs. Feasibility Study

Core Rule: "A thesis is a theory, and a feasibility study is the practical justification of applying the basic idea." If there is no theoretical claim to test, there is no thesis.

The Thesis (Academic Theory)

1
Tests a theoretical claim via empirical hypothesis testing (H0).
2
Requires causality and extends human knowledge.
Ex
Proving a new routing algorithm reduces packet loss by 20%.

The Feasibility Study (Viability)

1
Assesses whether a project can be practically & commercially implemented.
2
Forecasts financial returns, supply, demand, and competitors.
Ex
Forecasting ROI and competitor impact of opening a paid car park.

Step 2 of 8 • Problem Conceptualization

The Problem Funnel & Right vs. Wrong Computing Path

1. RESEARCH TOPIC (Broad Subject Area) — e.g., Distance Learning
2. RESEARCH PROBLEM (Specific Conflict or Gap) — e.g., Low student attendance in online sessions
3. PURPOSE STATEMENT (Major Objective) — e.g., To study why students skip live virtual classes
4. RESEARCH QUESTIONS (Exact Testable Inquiries) — e.g., Does platform usability deter attendance?

The Wrong Path (Tool-First Pitfall)

1
Spot a vague issue from initial reading.
2
Jump straight to choosing a Software Tool before defining the problem.
3
Scramble retroactively for literature just to defend the pre-chosen tool.

The Right Path (Two-Stage RRL)

1
Literature Review 1: Define the exact Research Problem from documented gaps.
2
Literature Review 2: Survey existing algorithms/solutions to avoid duplication.
3
Proposed Solution → Tool: Build the Software Tool as the implementation of your solution.
5 Titling Rules, 7 Selection Factors & 5-Part Problem Statement Flow
1
5 Titling Rules:
• Formulate the research problem before the title
• Keep the title 12 words or fewer
• Eliminate wasted phrases like "A Study of..." or "An Approach to..."
• Use a single or double title
• Include the core focus of the study
2
7 Problem Selection Considerations:
1. Interest (sustains motivation)
2. Magnitude (manageable scope)
3. Measurement of concepts (clear indicators)
4. Level of expertise (researcher competence)
5. Relevance (adds value to discipline)
6. Availability of data (accessible records)
7. Ethical issues (protects participants)
3
5-Part Flow of Ideas in the Problem Statement:
1. Research Topic (broad subject area)
2. Research Problem (specific issue or concern)
3. Justification from Literature (evidence that the issue matters)
4. Deficiencies in Prior Evidence (what prior studies missed)
5. Target Audience (who benefits from the study)
4
Purpose Statement Variable Ordering:
• Quantitative: 1st: Independent Variable (IV) → 2nd: Dependent Variable (DV) → 3rd: Control/Mediating Variable.
• Qualitative: Explores a single Central Phenomenon via Central, Sub- (Issue & Procedural), and Interview questions.

The 4 Levels of Measurement (NOIR Staircase — Lowest to Most Sensitive):

1. Nominal Names / Labels only (no order). Ex: Sex (1=M, 2=F), Civil Status, Party.
2. Ordinal Rank-ordered, but unequal distance between ranks. Ex: 1st, 2nd, 3rd place.
3. Interval Equal distance between units, but no absolute zero. Ex: Celsius (20°C, 30°C).
4. Ratio Equal intervals + True Absolute Zero (ratios valid). Ex: Typing speed (40 wpm = 2× 20 wpm), Weight, Age.

Step 5 of 8 • Hypothesis Testing & Risk

Null (H0) vs. Research (H1) & Interactive Error Simulator

Null Hypothesis (H0)

1
Statement of equality ("no difference / no relationship").
2
Refers to the population and is indirectly tested.
3
Written in Greek symbols (μ1 = μ2); implied benchmark.
4
Formulated to examine to refute, never to "prove".

Research Hypothesis (H1)

1
Statement of inequality (Non-directional "different from" or Directional "greater than").
2
Refers to the sample and is directly tested.
3
Written in Roman symbols (X̄1 ≠ X̄2); explicit claim.
4
Must be declarative, grounded in theory, brief, and testable.

Interactive Bell Curve Visualizer & P-Value Formatting Rules

Very Strong
.001 – .01
Strong (Good)
.01 – .05
Fair Evidence
.05 – .10
Weak / Marginal
P > .10
No Evidence
1
Always italicize and capitalize P.
2
Omit the leading zero before the decimal (P = .04, never 0.04) because P, α, and β cannot equal 1.
3
Report the exact value (P = .04) rather than an inequality, and never write "NS".
4
Express to 2 decimal digits if P > .01, or 3 decimal digits if P < .01.
5
If statistical software outputs .000, always write P < .001.
